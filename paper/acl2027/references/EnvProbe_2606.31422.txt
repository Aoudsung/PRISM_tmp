Introduction

Long-horizon language agents enable autonomous
decision-making in complex environments by planning and executing sequences of interdependent
actions. Building on reasoning-and-acting frameworks such as ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), reflectionbased agents such as Reflexion (Shinn et al.,
2023), and search-based planning methods such
as LATS (Zhou et al., 2024a), these agents increasingly support complex interactions with external
environments. Their capabilities extend from tool
use (Schick et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2024) to embodied decision-making (Huang et al., 2022; Ahn et al.,
2022) and long-horizon tasks evaluated in ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2021), ScienceWorld (Wang
et al., 2022), WebArena (Zhou et al., 2024b), and
UltraHorizon (Luo et al., 2025). However, reliable
long-horizon execution requires more than generating appropriate actions at individual steps, as effective planning depends on anticipating future environment states (Zhang et al., 2026). Agents must
maintain an accurate world model that represents
the evolving environment and supports subsequent
planning decisions, as demonstrated by Reasoning via Planning (RAP) (Hao et al., 2023). Such a
model allows agents to track relevant environmental states, reason about action preconditions, and
coordinate dependencies across multiple stages of
a task (Hill, 2025).
A fixed world model becomes increasingly unreliable during long-horizon execution as environmental changes render stored beliefs outdated. In
interactive environments such as ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2021) and WebArena (Zhou et al., 2024b),
agents may continue relying on states that no longer
reflect the environment, leading to discrepancies
between their internal beliefs and actual conditions (Yin and Du, 2026). For example, an agent
may assume that a tool remains available after its

Long-horizon language agents enable autonomous decision-making in complex environments by planning and executing sequences
of interdependent actions. To act reliably, these
agents must maintain an accurate world model
that tracks the environment’s evolving state and
supports subsequent decisions. However, as
interactions accumulate, stored beliefs can become outdated or incorrect, causing agents to
plan and act on assumptions that no longer reflect the environment. Such errors are difficult to resolve through additional reasoning or
self-reflection alone, because the information
needed for correction must be obtained from
the environment. Actively probing the environment therefore offers a direct way to verify and
correct these beliefs. To this end, we introduce
E NV P ROBE, a framework that enables agents
to actively explore their environments and correct inaccurate beliefs during long-horizon execution. Specifically, E NV P ROBE prioritizes
belief fields for verification based on task relevance and potential errors. It then selectively
probes the environment to retrieve the actual
value of a chosen field and incorporates the observation into the agent’s world model. With
the updated beliefs, the agent revises its plan
and continues execution based on the newly
acquired environmental information. Extensive
experiments across three controlled environments demonstrate that E NV P ROBE substantially improves terminal world-state accuracy
compared with agents operating without environment probing, leading to more reliable longhorizon decision-making. These results demonstrate the importance of actively verifying and
correcting beliefs through environmental interactions for maintaining accurate world models
in long-horizon language agents. Our code is
available at https://github.com/Hik289/
Environment-reduce-error.git.

the planning process to obtain direct evidence about

This limitation motivates actively acquiring information from the environment to verify and correct the agent’s beliefs. Rather than relying exclusively on previously observed states, an agent can
interact with its environment to determine whether
a relevant belief remains valid. For instance, it may
inspect the current state of a tool before invoking
a dependent operation or revisit a location before
planning its next movement. Such interactions provide direct evidence for updating the world model
and enable subsequent decisions to reflect the environment’s actual state. However, environmental
verification introduces an additional decision problem: agents must determine which beliefs require
verification and when such verification should occur. Because each probe consumes an interaction
step, unnecessary verification can reduce the opportunities available for task execution. This creates
a fundamental trade-off between acquiring infor2

Confidence calibration has been studied in neural networks (Guo et al., 2017) and language
models (Kadavath et al., 2022). UoT (Hu et al.,
2024) uses uncertainty to guide question generation, InfoSeeker (Fang and Ke, 2025) explores information gathering under partial observability, and
ReSpAct (Dongre et al., 2024) incorporates clarification actions. However, model-reported uncertainty may be unreliable when incorrect beliefs are

• We propose E NV P ROBE, an environmentprobing framework that selectively interacts with

• We develop a theoretical analysis that characterizes the benefits of environment probing for belief correction and its interaction costs, revealing
distinct effects on procedural and spatial beliefs.

and investigating the effects of different probing

Related Work

Language agents and world models. Longhorizon language agents combine reasoning, planning, and environment interaction to accomplish
complex tasks. ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) interleaves
reasoning with actions, Reflexion (Shinn et al.,
2023) incorporates feedback, and LATS (Zhou
et al., 2024a) introduces search-based planning.
Toolformer (Schick et al., 2023) and ToolLLM (Qin
et al., 2024) enable external tool use, while embodied agents (Huang et al., 2022; Ahn et al., 2022)
and Voyager (Wang et al., 2024) support environment interaction and persistent memory. Recent
work on memory-environment realignment (Yin
and Du, 2026) and rule-augmented memory (Yuan
et al., 2026) further addresses belief consistency.
However, these approaches do not explicitly study
budgeted environment probing for correcting individual belief fields during long-horizon execution.
